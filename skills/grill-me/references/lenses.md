# Question lenses

A menu, not a checklist. Mix freely, in either mode. There is no required count and no subset tied to a domain. **Do not name the lens out loud**; keep the conversation natural. The lens shapes the kind of question, not whether you ask it.

Adapt to the domain (coding, marketing, branding, SOPs, business decisions): read what the work actually is and let that shape what you probe.

- **First principles.** "If you started from zero, with no existing tools, audience, or code, would you still do it this way?"
- **Intent and desired outcome.** What does winning look like for the user personally, beyond the stated success criteria?
- **Constraint surfacing.** What is non-negotiable? Time, money, energy, values, identity. The real design lives in the constraints.
- **Hidden assumption.** "You said X. What has to be true for X to hold?"
- **Second-best alternative.** What path are they not taking? If they cannot name it, they have not chosen.
- **Pre-mortem.** "It is 12 months from now and this failed. Walk me through why."
- **Steelman the opposite.** Make the strongest case against the plan. If they cannot answer it, conviction is shallow.
- **Audience.** Who is this for? Name one person. What do they think, fear, want?
- **Reversibility.** One-way door or two-way door? They are designed differently.
- **Five whys.** "Why does that matter?" until you reach a value, an identity, or a non-negotiable.
- **Boundary.** What is out of scope? Naming what you will not do often clarifies more than what you will.
- **Sustainability.** Would they still do this if it took three times as long?
- **Anti-goals.** What would make this a failure even if it technically works?
- **Prior art.** What have they seen elsewhere that they loved or hated? Their reaction shows what they want.
- **Edge-case scenario.** Invent a specific case that sits on the boundary between two concepts and ask what should happen.

You may also adopt established frames without naming the source: permissionless leverage, "what do you believe that nobody agrees with", the value equation, jobs-to-be-done, regret minimization, inversion, pre-commitment, "what does the customer value?", "what are we optimizing for?". Adopt the frame, not the brand.
